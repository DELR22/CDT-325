Section 01

Introduction

Assignment 04 drew the building in plan. Assignment 05 designs its ceiling and its floor.

The ceiling is where light fixtures, HVAC diffusers, sprinklers, and exit signs land. They compete for the same plane, and fitting them together is the design challenge. You also decide what the ceiling is made of and how high it sits.

The floor is where you specify materials and where they change. Every one of those lines is a decision.

Both surfaces do more work for this population than they would elsewhere. Older eyes need more light and are more sensitive to glare, so fixture type and ceiling height determine how well a resident sees. A high-contrast floor pattern can read as a hole or a step to someone with low vision. A threshold between two materials is where a walker wheel catches.

You will select real products from commercial manufacturers' catalogs, document where each goes, and demonstrate the result in three rendered spaces with the specified furniture.

You Bring

  • Assignment 04: Sheets 1 and 2
  • Assignment 03: Area Program and code requirements
  • Assignment 02: Concept Statement
  • Assignment 01: Primary and Secondary Source research and collective library
The objective. Design and document the ceiling and floor for the whole project, selecting real materials from real manufacturers’ catalogs, and demonstrate the results in three rendered spaces with the specified FF&E.

Section 02

Why Real Manufacturers & Objectives

The point of using manufacturers is twofold:

  • Choose finishes that serve your concept.
  • Understand how ceiling and flooring systems actually deploy — their module, their direction, their seams — and what that means for the rooms you drew.

Objectives

  1. Select commercial ceiling and flooring materials that serve the design concept.
  2. Documenting material distribution through hatch conventions represents how each material is deployed.
  3. Coordinate ceiling heights, luminaires, HVAC, sprinklers, and exit signs on a single plane.
  4. Lay out a sprinkler grid to NFPA spacing limits.
  5. Communicate the design through legends and symbol keys.
  6. Prove the design in three rendered spaces with specified FF&E.

Material & Hatch Conventions

Material definition. Select ceiling materials from the USG catalog. Choose finishes that serve your concept, then draw where each one goes.

Hatch conventions must represent the real material — not an abstract pattern. If a tile deploys at 24″ × 24″, the hatch reads 24″ × 24″ at a 1/8″ scale. If the system is linear, the hatch appears as parallel lines at the real width and in the intended direction.

What Must Appear

  • Ceiling heights, specified for every space.
  • Light fixtures / luminaires. Each type of fixture must be represented by a particular symbol that matches a legend key. Select luminaire types from Delta Light’s product browser.
  • Ceiling-mounted exit signs, coordinated with your A04 Sheet 2.
  • HVAC grilles & diffusers. Select types from Inviair’s visual applications.
  • Sprinklers, with a dashed coverage radius at each head.
    • The sprinkler grid. Lay out heads to NFPA 13 spacing for standard spray sprinklers in a maximum-spaced grid of 15′-0″ x 15′-0″. If a room is smaller than 15′-0″ x 15′-0″, it must have at least one sprinkler.
    • Minimum distance from a wall is 4″.
    • Show each head’s coverage radius dashed, which describes circles 15′-0″ in diameter.
  • Legend & symbol key. A complete key covering every type of luminaire and every object embedded in or hanging from the ceiling — diffusers, grilles, sprinklers, exit signs. Use the conventions in the template. Do not invent symbols.

Section 06

Stage 2 · Floor Finish Plan

Sheet 4 · First and Second Floors · Scale 1/8″ = 1′-0″

  • Material definition. Select flooring from the Mohawk Group catalog to serve your concept and draw where each material goes.
  • The same hatch rule applies, and it matters more here. Flooring deployment is visible to the occupant in a way that ceiling deployment is not. If a carpet tile deploys at 24″ × 24″, the hatch reads 24″ × 24″. If a plank deploys in parallel boards, the hatch reads as parallel boards, running in the intended direction.
  • Transitions. Every place two materials meet is a decision. Show the transitions with elements that mediate between the two surface types — these could be:
    • Thresholds
    • An overlapping linear element in a continuous space
  • Legend & symbol key. Every material must be included in the legend key, referencing its type and name.

Section 09

Evaluation Rubric

The assignment is evaluated across four criteria totaling 300 points. Ceiling Design & Coordination carries the greatest weight, as fitting every system onto a single plane is the core technical work of Stage 1.

Criterion Excellent Proficient Developing
1. Ceiling Design & Coordination 100 pts total
90 – 100 pts
Coordinates the ceiling plane with rigor: resolves every space by specifying heights that serve their zone, distributing materials, and placing luminaires, exit signs, grilles, and diffusers alongside a sprinkler grid that holds its spacing.
70 – 89 pts
Coordinates the ceiling plane competently: resolves the required spaces by specifying heights and distributing materials, and by placing luminaires, devices, and a sprinkler grid, leaving isolated conflicts or omissions between systems.
0 – 69 pts
Coordinates the ceiling plane loosely: leaves it unresolved by omitting heights, elements, or the sprinkler grid, by exceeding its spacing limits, and by placing fixtures that collide with the systems sharing the plane.
2. Floor Design & Material Definition 80 pts total
72 – 80 pts
Defines the floor with precision: distributes materials that serve the concept by hatching each at its dimension and direction, and by resolving every place where two materials meet with a threshold or mediating element.
56 – 71 pts
Defines the floor competently: distributes materials that suit the concept by hatching them at approximately their real deployment and by showing the principal transitions, leaving some hatches abstract or some junctions unresolved.
0 – 55 pts
Defines the floor superficially: distributes materials by hatching them in abstract patterns unrelated to their deployment, and leaves the transitions between them undrawn.
3. Renderings & FF&E Specification 70 pts total
63 – 70 pts
Demonstrates the design convincingly: proves that the chosen materials perform by rendering representative spaces and by specifying every FF&E item and luminaire with complete manufacturer data keyed to the room numbers.
49 – 62 pts
Demonstrates the design adequately: proves that the chosen materials perform by rendering representative spaces and by specifying the principal items with manufacturer data, leaving fields incomplete or keys inconsistent.
0 – 48 pts
Demonstrates the design unconvincingly: presents views that contradict the material selection by specifying items without coherent or coordinated data.
4. Documentation, Legends & Concept Continuity 50 pts total
45 – 50 pts
Documents to technical standards: makes the drawings readable by keying every ceiling-mounted object and material to a complete legend in the template’s conventions, and by accounting for the material choices as decisions the concept and the research required.
35 – 44 pts
Documents clearly: makes the drawings readable by keying the required elements to legends and by connecting the material choices to the concept, with minor inconsistencies.
0 – 34 pts
Documents poorly: obscures the drawings by leaving legends incomplete, by inventing symbols outside the template, and by describing what was selected rather than why the concept required it.
Total 270 – 300 pts 210 – 266 pts 0 – 206 pts
