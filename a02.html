Section 01

Context, What You Bring Forward & Objectives

In Assignment 01, you learned about a population from the population itself. You have interviews, secondary research, an assigned topic, and a Grounded Design Insight — something you could only know from doing the fieldwork.

Research does not turn into design on its own. Someone has to translate it. That is this assignment.

Part 1 turns your research into a Concept Statement — the argument your interior design will make. Part 2 turns that statement into Mood Environments — images that show what the concept looks and feels like in terms of material, space, and light. Together, they become the brief you design from.

One Rule Governs Everything

Every claim you enter must come from a person you actually spoke with.

What You Bring From Assignment 01

  1. Your four interviews.
  2. Your assigned topic and its secondary research.
  3. Your Grounded Design Insight.
  4. The collective library — your classmates’ twenty-four other topics.

Objectives

  1. Translate interview evidence into a structured understanding of your users.
  2. Reframe user challenges as design opportunities.
  3. Write a Concept Statement that can direct real design decisions.
  4. Produce Mood Environments that give the project material, spatial, and aesthetic direction.
  5. Present the concept in a professional environment.

Section 02

Student Learning Outcomes

Bloom’s Taxonomy (2001)

  • Remember Recall the interview evidence and secondary research gathered in Assignment 01.
  • Understand Explain user needs and desires, as well as technical requirements, to impact design decisions.
  • Apply Use the primary and secondary sources to transfer research into a design concept.
  • Analyze Compare user accounts and theoretical information to identify what they share and where they conflict.
  • Evaluate Judge which design ideas work best and which conceptual images serve the concept.
  • Create Produce a Concept Statement and a set of Mood Environments that give the project a coherent direction and present them to a client.

CIDA / NASAD Framework

  • Apply Human-centered evidence to define the users of a space and what they need from it.
  • Synthesize User research into design intent, identifying shared needs, conflicts, and what the design must do.
  • Work Through a design process, framing problems as opportunities, generating ideas, and selecting the strongest.
  • Give A concept, material, spatial, and aesthetic direction through conceptual imagery that applies light, color, texture, and material as design elements.
  • Present And defend a design concept to a client through professional visual and oral communication.
  • Collaborate As a professional team and document the design process ethically, protecting participant privacy.

Part 1

The Concept Statement

Working from your Assignment 01 interviews, you will build a profile on user preferences for each of your four participants, your Design Insights, and synthesize the DOs and DON’Ts of your final users into a single composite understanding of who you are designing for, generate and select design ideas, and arrive at a Concept Statement that describes the project’s Value Proposition and Core Design Solution.

Before you begin, watch the instructor’s video tutorias, then use the Concept Statement Generator web app, sites.ua.edu/dronderos/concept-statement-generator, as a guide that tells you what to put into it and why.

You Must Produce

  • One DOs & DON’Ts synthesis
  • Design ideas generated & selected, selections marked
One Concept Statement Value Proposition + Core Design Solution
02
Part 2

Mood Environments

Completed using the DREAMS + EP web app — sites.ua.edu/dronderos/d-r-e-a-m-s. As in Part 1, watch the instructor’s video tutorial before you begin.

Working from the Concept Statement, use the Value Proposition and Core Design Solution to identify different feelings, perceptions, or experiences that the project must create for your users. Afterward, translate at least three into spatial diagrams of place, sensation, material, objects, surroundings, and light, and convert each diagram into an AI image-generation prompt. You will then generate, edit, and curate the results according to the instructor’s video tutorials.

You Must Produce

  • 3 feeling sets
  • 3 DREAMS canvases
  • 3 edited image prompts
  • 3 Mood Environments

Section 04

Deliverables

Deliverable 01 · 5–10 Minutes · Live · Delivered as a Design Firm
Concept Presentation

Your firm. Choose a name, design a logo, write a slogan.

What you present. Take the client from your research to your concept, then show the concept as images:

  • Who your users are — the four people you interviewed, characterized as you did when using the Concept Statement Generator App.
  • How you built the concept — the user preference profiles, your Design Insights, the DOs and DON’Ts synthesis, the ideas you generated and the ones you kept.
  • The Concept Statement — your Value Proposition and Core Design Solution.

The visual argument. Present several conceptual renderings from your Mood Environments. Do not narrate them one at a time — explain why they belong together. Your images must establish one spatial, material, and aesthetic universe.

How you present. Choose a maximum of two speakers. It does not matter whether everyone talks; it matters that the speech is coherent, well delivered, and carries the project’s conceptual core. Two voices with one argument beat four voices with four.

This is a client presentation to NEXA Consulting and Development. Speak to the client. Do not read from slides. Do not exceed ten minutes. Be professional.

Deliverable 02 · Cumulative · On the Collaborative Board
Project Memory Log

Each group has a space on a shared collaborative board provided by the instructor. Build a chronological record of how the concept came to exist — screenshots from every stage of both parts, in order, from your first user profile through your last curated image. Include the ideas you set aside, the prompts you rewrote, and the images you rejected.

Why is the board shared? Your log sits alongside every other group’s. Learn from other groups. Read your classmates’ boards.

Never post a participant’s real name, photograph, or voice recording. The board is shared with the class. Your consent agreement is not.

Submission

  1. In-person presentation. All group members must be in the classroom, including those who do not speak.
  2. Corporate Presentation file, including the firm’s name, logo, and slogan.
  3. PDF of the Concept Statement — Value Proposition + Core Design Solution, as the app produced it. Do not rewrite it.
  4. Project Memory entry on the collaborative board, at the access link provided by the instructor.
Items 2, 3, and 4 are submitted through Blackboard. Presentation dates and deadlines are on the course syllabus.

Section 05

Evaluation Rubric

The assignment is evaluated across four criteria totaling 200 points. Concept Development & the Concept Statement carries the greatest weight, as it is where research becomes an actionable design argument.

Criterion Excellent Proficient Developing
1. Research Translation & User Synthesis 60 pts total
54 – 60 pts
Applies human-centered evidence with rigor: defines specific users and their needs by comprehensively synthesizing shared needs and conflicts into design intent.
42 – 53 pts
Applies human-centered evidence effectively: defines plausible users and their needs by synthesizing shared needs into design intent while resolving conflicts.
0 – 41 pts
Applies human-centered evidence loosely: defines assumed users by superficially averaging separate accounts into a single undifferentiated intent.
2. Concept Development & the Concept Statement 70 pts total
63 – 70 pts
Commands the design process: frames problems as genuinely open opportunities by generating ideas freely and selecting the strongest into an interdependent, verifiable concept.
49 – 62 pts
Works through the design process competently: frames problems as opportunities by generating ideas and selecting soundly into a coherent but loosely bound concept.
0 – 48 pts
Bypasses the design process: frames problems that contain their own answers by generating few ideas and selecting by appeal into an unsupported concept.
3. Mood Environments & Visual Direction 40 pts total
36 – 40 pts
Gives the concept direction with precision: commands light, color, texture, and material by curating conceptual imagery into one cohesive spatial universe answerable to its users.
28 – 35 pts
Adequately gives the concept direction: applies light, color, texture, and material by curating conceptual imagery whose language shifts between sets.
0 – 27 pts
Gives the concept no discernible direction: disregards light, color, texture, and material by assembling imagery that neither coheres nor serves the concept.

27 – 30 pts

21 – 26 pts
Presents and defends the concept clearly: collaborates effectively by documenting the process completely and protecting participant privacy.
0 – 20 pts
Presents the concept without authority: collaborates unevenly by documenting the process incompletely and exposing participant identity.
