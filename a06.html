Sections

Section 01

Introduction & Objectives

Assignment 06 is the final stage of the process: communicating the project and its features through static and dynamic visual documents. It packages the work already done into a client presentation.

Everything you produced in Assignments 01 through 05 is the project’s design process, from its conceptual approach to detailed material specifications. The challenge now is to build a coherent narrative for your client and the project’s stakeholders.

To do this, reflect on your work so far and create compelling deliverables that explain the project conceptually and technically, using evocative static and dynamic visual and audiovisual elements.

Objectives

  1. Synthesize an entire design process into audiovisual and graphic elements.
  2. Argue for a design proposal to a client audience, concisely and convincingly.
  3. Present professionally under a corporate identity.
  4. Communicate research, concept, and design resolution to non-designers.

Section 02

Deliverables · The Posters

This assignment consists of three deliverables: two posters and one recorded video pitch. This section covers the posters.

Produce two 36″ × 48″ posters that explain the design process and the project’s features, and show compliance with Building Code, ADA Standards, and Fire/Life Safety design measures.

What You Can Include

Include relevant information from the documents you produced during the design process, including, but not limited to:

i
User Interviews & Research
ii
Design Concept Development
  • Concept Statement: Value Proposition + Core Design Solutions, and how these evolved through the different stages of development.
  • Conceptual mood environment development.
iii
Area Planning
  • Bubble Diagrams
  • Block Plans
iv
Design Documentation
  • Interior Design Floor Plans
  • Finish Plans
  • Renderings, including Material Board development
  • Specifications
Important note: What to include in the posters is your decision as the designer. Decide what is relevant to show to fully explain the project, its design process, and its code-compliant features.
3
Deliverable 3 The Video Pitch

Record a 5- to 7-minute video pitch presenting the project to NEXA’s board. The goal is not only to explain the project, but mainly to convince the board members that your design proposal is the best alternative.

What to include in the video is your decision as the designer. However, it should not repeat information already shown on the posters. Turning the video into an audiovisual retelling of the design process and its technical solutions may be counterproductive.

This is a professional sales pitch, not an academic exercise.

Evaluation Rubric

The assignment is evaluated across four criteria totaling 200 points. The two poster criteria carry the greatest combined weight, as the posters are the core of the proposal.

Criterion Excellent Proficient Developing
1. Posters — Design Process 60 pts total
54 – 60 pts
Selects the relevant parts of the process (user research, the Concept Statement and how it evolved, mood environments, and area planning) and presents them as one coherent narrative that a non-designer can follow.
42 – 53 pts
Presents the process clearly, but the selection is uneven, or the evolution of the concept is stated rather than shown.
0 – 41 pts
Reproduces documents without a narrative, leaves out key stages, or is hard for a non-designer to follow.

54 – 60 pts

0 – 41 pts
Presents features that are unclear or illegible, or leaves out compliance with Building Code, ADA Standards, or Fire/Life Safety.

27 – 30 pts

Retells the design process like an academic exercise, misses the time limit, or presents without a professional identity.

Eileen Gray (1878–1976)

Eileen Gray was an Irish-born furniture designer and architect who worked mostly in France. She moved from lacquer work to furniture, interiors, and finally architecture, and she is now recognized as one of the pioneers of modern design, after decades in which her work was overlooked or credited to others.

Training and Early Career

  • Education: Studied painting at the Slade School of Fine Art in London, where she first became interested in lacquer.
  • Paris: Settled in Paris in the early 1900s and learned traditional Japanese lacquer techniques with the craftsman Seizo Sugawara.
  • Jean Désert: In 1922 she opened her own gallery, Jean Désert, to sell her furniture, rugs, and lamps.

Furniture

  • Dragon armchair (c. 1917–1919): A lacquered chair whose arms curl like dragons. In 2009 it sold at auction for about €21.9 million, a record for a 20th-century design object.
  • Bibendum chair (c. 1926): A playful armchair built from stacked padded tubes.
  • E-1027 side table (1927): An adjustable tubular steel table designed so a guest could have breakfast in bed. It is still in production.

Architecture

  • E-1027 (1926–1929): A seaside villa at Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, France, designed with Jean Badovici. She designed the house and its furniture together, around how people would actually live in it.
  • Tempe à Pailla (1930s): Her own house in Castellar, France, with furniture that folds, slides, and adapts to small spaces.

Why She Matters for Your Pitch

Gray believed that a building is a container for human life, not an object to be admired. Her designs start with the person who will use them: where they sit, sleep, eat, and move. That is the same argument your pitch must make to the NEXA board: what the design does for the people who will live and learn in it.
