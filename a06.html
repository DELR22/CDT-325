Section 01

Introduction

Assignment 06 produces no new design. It packages the work you have already done into a form a client can act on.

Everything from Assignments 01 through 05 is currently coursework. This assignment consists of three deliverables: two 24″ × 36″ posters, and a recorded 5- to 7-minute pitch asking NEXA’s board to build it.

Both are proposals, not summaries. A poster is not a portfolio page and a pitch is not a narration of your process. Each makes the same case in a different medium.

What Is Yours, and What Is Your Design Team’s

You worked as part of a design team through Assignment 02 and alone from Assignment 03 forward. The deliverables carry your design team’s identity (name, logo, and slogan) and both present your individual design.

The research, the Concept Statement, and the brand are your team’s. Every design decision from the block plan forward is yours. You may use the group’s work, but you may not present a classmate’s interior design proposal.

Objectives

  1. Synthesize an entire design process into audiovisual and graphic elements.
  2. Argue for a design proposal to a client audience, concisely and convincingly.
  3. Present professionally under a corporate identity.
  4. Communicate research, concept, and design resolution to non-designers.

Section 02

Deliverables 1 & 2 · The Project Posters

Two boards · 24″ × 36″ each · Built in Adobe Photoshop

To build these posters, follow the video tutorials available on Panopto for instructions on using Photoshop to create posters. Requirement: download Photoshop on your computer.
Board 1 · The Argument for the Design
Research & Concept

Open with your primary source research — what the people you interviewed told you about the spaces in their lives — told through the quotes and discoveries that actually drove your concept, the sentence someone said that changed how you saw the problem, rather than as a methodology summary; explain the findings, conclusions, and insights that worked, and anchor the project to the final users.

Carry that into your secondary source research, the theories, standards, and precedents you drew from the collective knowledge video library, and then into your Concept Development, showing how that research became a concept and presenting the concept itself: your Value Proposition and Core Design Solution.

Close the board with your Mood Environments, the three conceptual image sets that ground the concept visually.

Board 2 · The Design Itself
The Proposal

Present the design itself, beginning with rendered floor plans, reduced in size but legible and carrying a graphic scale, including textured representations of floor finishes and furniture, followed by material mood boards for your ceiling and flooring selections and your FF&E.

Close with as many renderings of the proposed spaces as you can produce, since this is where the building becomes real to someone who does not read plans.

Deliverable 3 The Executive Pitch

You are presenting to the NEXA Consulting and Development board. They are not designers, they have not read your assignments, and they are deciding whether to build your project — so use the research as evidence for your design, show the building, and tell them what it does for the people who will live and learn in it.

This is not a recount of your design process. It is a proposal. Be professional, direct, concise, and convincing.

Section 05

Evaluation Rubric

The assignment is evaluated across four criteria totaling 200 points. Board 1 and Board 2 carry the greatest combined weight, as the posters are the core of the proposal.

Criterion Excellent Proficient Developing
1. Board 1 — Research & Concept 60 pts total
54 – 60 pts
Presents primary findings and user quotes, secondary sources from the collective library, and a concept that follows from both. The connection to the users is clear throughout.
42 – 53 pts
Presents the research and the concept, but states the link between them rather than showing it, or ties the design loosely to the users.
0 – 41 pts
Summarizes the process, or presents findings that are disconnected from the concept and not tied to any user.
2. Board 2 — The Proposal 60 pts total
54 – 60 pts
Presents rendered floor plans that remain legible at reduced size with a graphic scale, textured finishes and furniture, material mood boards for ceiling, floor, and FF&E, and enough renderings to convey the spaces to a non-designer.
42 – 53 pts
Presents legible plans, the material selections, and renderings that convey the design, with occasional lapses in legibility, scale, or completeness.
0 – 41 pts
Presents plans that lose legibility or omit the scale, limited material information, or too few renderings to convey the design.
3. Graphic System & Firm Identity 30 pts total
27 – 30 pts
Composes both boards as one consistent style and graphic system with a clear reading order, and legible.
21 – 26 pts
Composes two coordinated boards with the firm identity, but with an uneven reading order or occasional legibility lapses.
0 – 20 pts
Composes boards that read as two separate projects, lack a clear reading order, or fall below legibility standards.
4. The Executive Pitch 50 pts total
45 – 50 pts
Pitches the design in the required time. Leads with the proposal, uses research as evidence, explains what the design does for its users, and presents professionally under the firm’s identity with legible on-screen support.
35 – 44 pts
Pitches the project within time under the firm’s identity and conveys the design clearly, but narrates process at points or argues less than convincingly.
0 – 34 pts
Recounts the process instead of pitching, misses the time limit, or presents without firm identity or legible support.
Total 180 – 200 pts 140 – 176 pts 0 – 136 pts
