Section 01

What This Course Is About

This studio is built on the conviction that design for people unlike us cannot begin with the designer’s imagination. It must begin with the humility to ask, listen, and be corrected by the people who will live with the result — which is why the semester opens not with drawing but with fieldwork, governed by the principle “nothing about us without us.”

From that ethical foundation, the course advances a single project through the full arc of practice — research into concept, concept into plan, plan into resolved and specified documentation — so that students experience design not as a single creative leap but as a continuous act of translation and iteration, where every decision answers to a real person’s input and experience, and to the codes, accessibility standards, and human factors that make a building safe and usable for specific populations with specific needs.

Nothing About Us Without Us

The pedagogy is deliberately cumulative and progressively focused. Students first think and present as a collaborative firm in an empathic, research-guided phase, then assume sole authorship of technical documents that must preserve every research insight and design idea while resolving the functional demands of the area program and the building code.

A defensible process matters more than a polished output — methodological integrity is the competitive and disciplinary edge.

Section 02

Pedagogical Approach

The Interior Design profession does not reward those who can only follow instructions or operate tools; it rewards those who can enter an unfamiliar system, adapt to its standards, and carry an idea responsibly all the way to something functional, buildable, and meaningful. These are the pillars that recur across every assignment.

Pillar 01

Fieldwork Before Design

The semester opens with primary-source interviews, not drawing. Community members are not subjects to be studied — they are the authorities on their own experience, and the co-informants of the design that follows.

Pillar 02

One Project, Full Arc

A single project advances through the entire arc of practice — research into concept, concept into plan, plan into resolved and specified documentation — so every later decision remains traceable back to its origin.

Pillar 03

From Firm to Individual Authorship

Students think and present as a collaborative firm through the empathic and conceptual phases, then assume sole authorship of the technical documents — carrying the team’s research forward under individual professional accountability.

Pillar 04

Process Over Polish

A defensible process matters more than a polished output. Rubrics reward decisions a student can explain and trace back to research, code, or user evidence — not surface finish alone.

Pillar 05

Codes & Human Factors as Determinants

Building codes, life-safety regulations, and accessibility standards are not a checklist applied after the fact — they are treated as design determinants that shape the plan from its earliest zoning.

Pillar 06

Real Client, Real Accountability

The project is developed for a real client, NEXA Consulting and Development, and presented as a professional firm would — defending decisions rather than simply describing them.

Section 03

Studio Culture & Critiques

Critiques are not a checkpoint bolted onto the schedule; they are how this studio verifies that a project is actually moving, and moving as the student’s own work. Progress is demonstrated in the room, during class time — not assembled the night before a deadline.

Project Development Commitment

Every student is expected to bring their project’s advances to class and to work on the project during class time. When called on, you must be able to present where your individual work currently stands.

Each time a student cannot present advances on their individual project when prompted, 5% is deducted from that assignment’s final grade. Missing class on a day advances are due triggers this deduction automatically.

The policy exists to structure the studio around autonomy and sustained commitment — a shared critique culture only works if every student consistently arrives with something to critique.

Section 04

Student Learning Outcomes

Students will be able to do the following, each demonstrated through specific assignments across the semester.

  • SLO 01 Human-Centered Research & Ethical Engagement — Conduct primary research directly with users as authorities on their own experience, locate and weigh relevant secondary research, and synthesize the two into a defensible, fieldwork-dependent design insight. Demonstrated in: Understanding the Final User (A01).
  • SLO 02 Design Thinking & Concept Development — Work through a design process to reframe user challenges as opportunities, generate and select ideas, and translate research into a Design Concept and an aesthetic, material, and spatial direction that remains legible from schematic zoning through fully resolved documentation. Demonstrated in: Concept Statement & Mood Environments (A02).
  • SLO 03 Programming & Space Planning — Translate a quantified area program into a coherent spatial scheme of zones, areas, adjacencies, and circulation that serves the concept and its users, while resolving the functional aspects of Human-Centered Interior Design. Demonstrated in: The Block Plan (A03).
  • SLO 04 Codes, Life-Safety & Regulatory Application — Apply building codes and life-safety regulations as design determinants to produce professional-standard-compliant technical design documents. Demonstrated in: The Block Plan (A03) and Design Documents (A04).
  • SLO 05 Universal Design, Accessibility & Human Factors — Apply ADA and universal-design standards, anthropometrics, and human-factors reasoning to produce functional, inclusive, and safe spaces for a population with specific needs and requirements. Demonstrated in: The Block Plan (A03) and Design Documents (A04).
  • SLO 06 Materials, Finishes & FF&E Specification — Select real products from commercial manufacturers’ catalogs, document how these systems actually deploy, and specify FF&E and luminaires with complete, coordinated manufacturer data that serves the concept and the people who will occupy the space. Demonstrated in: Additional Design Documentation (A05).
  • SLO 07 Technical Documentation & Multi-Platform Digital Production — Operate BIM, CAD, and render tools across a multi-platform workflow to produce standard-compliant documents within a predetermined template, applying professional drawing conventions. Demonstrated in: BIM Modeling Foundations (A00), Design Documents (A04), and Additional Design Documentation (A05).
  • SLO 08 Design Communication & Professional Practice — Communicate and defend design decisions through narrated, visual, and oral media, collaborating as a design team while exercising individual professional judgment and taking sole accountability for technical documents. Demonstrated in: Concept Statement & Mood Environments (A02), Project Presentation (A06), and every individually authored assignment thereafter.
