Section 02

What This Is, and What It Is Not

You are closing in on graduating or applying for internships. One of the most valuable things you can show a firm is not that you know one piece of software; it is that you have the ability to engage with a firm’s template, families, parameters, and graphic standards, and produce usable work. Every firm has its own system. None of them will teach you theirs from scratch. They hire people who can adapt.

That is what A00 trains. You will follow video tutorials that build a case-study project inside a Revit template with predefined parameters, families, view templates, and sheet templates. You will then carry those methods into your studio project on your own.

The gap between the tutorial building and your building is not an obstacle. It is the entire point. Following keystrokes on someone else’s project proves you can follow a video. Rebuilding the method on your own project proves you can transfer a skill, which is the thing a firm is actually buying.

So, the goal is not to become a Revit master. It is to gain the tools, methods, and habits to work inside predetermined BIM and graphic standards and produce functional models, 2D documentation, and 3D renderings. This is also a multi-platform workflow, and you will move information between AutoCAD and Revit at the points where each performs best.

Guiding Principle

Following keystrokes on someone else’s project proves you can follow a video. Rebuilding the method on your own project proves you can transfer a skill — the thing a firm is actually buying.

Bloom’s Taxonomy (2001)

  • Understand How predefined templates relate to standard-compliant project development.
  • Apply Revit modeling to place partitions, floors, ceilings, doors, and windows, and compose sheets using the template’s standards.
  • Analyze Family type parameters to accurately define partitions, floors, ceilings, doors, and windows.
  • Create Functional Revit models, 2D documentation, and 3D renderings from a template-compliant model.

CIDA / NASAD

  • Operate BIM and CAD software to produce 3D and 2D technical documentation.
  • Work Within predetermined graphic standards to produce consistent, plottable documents.
  • Execute A multi-platform workflow, moving a design from AutoCAD into Revit and from Revit into a rendering engine.
  • Model Building elements accurately — walls and glazing by type, floors, ceilings, doors, and windows — using the template’s definitions.
  • Produce Presentation renderings by exporting the model through a rendering pipeline.

Section 03

Tools & Setup

Revit version. The tutorials are recorded in Revit 2026. The computer labs run the latest version available, which your instructor will announce in class. The basic operations in these tutorials are consistent across versions, so the tutorials remain valid.

One rule about versions: a Revit file opens only in its own version or newer, never older. To work on lab machines, install or update to the same version as the labs. If you’d rather stay on your own computer, you may — as long as it runs Revit 2026 or newer — but a file you save in a newer version will not open on a computer with an older version. Match the labs’ version or keep your work entirely on your own machine.

Template. The course Revit template is on Blackboard. It is required and non-negotiable.

AutoCAD. Used in Module 1 for the block-plan-to-Revit workflow.

Rendering. Twinmotion (free for students), D5 Render (free educational license), or AI rendering — your choice. Check system requirements before installing Twinmotion or D5 Render.

Section 05

Academic Integrity

Every academic environment starts from the belief that people want to learn, so the honor system is in place from the beginning.

That trust rests on a simple fact: no two authentic files are identical. Two students working genuinely will make different choices, different small mistakes, different moves. If the same specific errors or characteristics appear in two or more submissions, the students involved will be held accountable, and an inquiry will follow, including a search of the file metadata to establish authorship.

Section 06

Evaluation Rubric

The assignment is evaluated across the three modules, totaling 200 points. Module 2 — Design Documentation — carries the greatest weight, as it is the core modeling work of the exercise.

Module (Weight) Excellent Proficient Developing
Module 1 — Block Plan: AutoCAD-to-Revit Model, Rooms & Annotations 60 pts total
54 – 60 pts
Executes the AutoCAD-to-Revit workflow cleanly, builds a fully room-bounded model by tagging every space with a name, number, and area, and composes the sheet on the provided title block exactly to the template’s standards.
42 – 53 pts
Executes the workflow competently, builds a room-based model by tagging the spaces and composing the sheet, with isolated bounding errors, missing tags, or minor departures from the template.
0 – 41 pts
Executes the workflow incompletely, builds a model by leaving rooms unbounded or untagged, or by composing the sheet off-template or off-size.
Module 2 — Design Documentation Model 90 pts total
82 – 90 pts
Models the building accurately, defines every element by placing walls and interior glazing as correctly rated and non-rated types, floors and ceilings with heights, and doors and windows with correct types and swings, all composed on sheets through the view and sheet templates.
64 – 81 pts
Models the building competently, defines the required elements by placing them largely by the correct type and composing them on template sheets, with isolated errors in type, height, or swing.
0 – 63 pts
Models the building loosely, defines elements using generic or incorrect wall types, omits floors, ceilings, or heights, misplaces doors and windows, and composes views outside the template.
Module 3 — 3D Rendering Pipeline 50 pts total
46 – 50 pts
Completes the rendering workflow, exports the model, produces all three renderings from the designated perspectives, and submits them with the model.
36 – 45 pts
Completes the workflow, produces the renderings from the designated perspectives with an incomplete set or a missing export.
0 – 35 pts
Leaves the workflow incomplete: submits fewer than three renderings, uses the wrong perspectives, or provides no model.
Total 200 points across Modules 1–3
