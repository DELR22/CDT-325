    Level 01 of 08

    Overview

    What Assignment 04 is, what you must do before starting, and what you produce.

    What It Is

    • Individual assignment.
    • Assignment 04 is the first of two stages to develop the project’s design documentation, based on your Assignment 03 Block Plan.
      • Do NOT create a new file for this stage. The project is a continuum that matures through iteration. The modifications done in this assignment update your Block Plan to maintain coherency.
      • Duplicate the First, Second, and Third Block Plans at the Floor Finish (FF) levels.
        • Name the duplicated views FF Level 1 DD, FF Level 2 DD, and FF Level 3 DD.
    • You include more elements in the project to obtain a higher Level of Detail (LOD).
    • Every space continues to be labeled with a name, a number, and square footage.
    • Every wall must be defined by its thickness and a fire rating.
    • Every room must have furniture and fixtures drawn at true size, in the same scale as the floor plan. Scaling furniture and fixtures is strictly prohibited.

    Before You Start

    • Assignment 04 is built directly on your Assignment 03 Block Plan.
    • Stage 1 uses your block plan’s geometry. Only non-substantial edits and adjustments are allowed.
    • Stage 2 documents the fire and life safety of that same plan.
    • Check your Block Plan for unresolved egress paths, missing compartment lines, and mixed public–resident circulation.
    • Fix every deficiency first, before you start Assignment 04.
    • If you do not, the problems carry into both stages and you will have to redo the work.

    What You Produce

    • Floor plans that show the design is fully resolved.
    • Show walls with ratings, doors with correct swings, furniture, and fixtures with clearances.
    • Use notes and dashed clearances. Do not use dimensions.
    • The FF&E is a reference for scale, proportion, and functionality. It is not for a contractor to build from.
    • Use basic Revit families. Do not spend time looking for detailed ones.

    Level 03 of 08

    Objectives & The Tools

    What the assignment asks you to do and what you bring to it.

    Objectives

    Level 04 of 08

    Walls, Windows & Doors

    Requirements for walls, windows, doors, and hallway elements.

    A · Walls & Partitions

    • Wall and vertical partition placement must continue to follow the same criteria inherited from the Block Plan.
    • If you create new walls, select materials based on the required fire rating.
      • Use 2-hour walls at main hallways, assembly areas, egress stairs, janitor’s closets, technical / mechanical rooms, and storage rooms.
      • Use 1-hour walls at shared or room-dividing partitions.
      • Use 0-hour walls at internal room partitions.
    • Add to the wall classification keys the type of wall assembly you choose for each fire rating. Use the USG Wall Assembly types and Fireglass catalogs.

    B · Windows & Glass Partitions

    • Draw all exterior windows and interior glass partitions (use the Fireglass catalog as a reference). Base them on the activities that take place inside each space.
      • Give glass partitions a fire rating where required (assembly spaces).
      • Specify fire-rated glass using Fireglass fire-rated products.

    C · Doors: Width, Swing, and Clearance

    • Draw every door with its swing, at its true width (use standardized Revit families).
    • Resident bedrooms: 42″ minimum (gurney access).
    • All other doors: 36″ minimum, with the exception of closets, which can be 28″ to 32″.
    • Swing exit doors in the direction of egress.
    • Swing assembly doors out, in the direction of egress, without intruding into the corridor. They must be recessed.
    • Compartment doors: a pair, where each individual door must be 42″ wide, each swinging in the opposite direction from the other.
    • Swing inward-opening doors against a wall or back surface, not into the space.
    • Outward-swinging doors may project no more than 7″ into a corridor.
    • Place doors at least 4″ from a back wall.
    • Leave 12″ on the push side and 18″ on the pull side.
      • Make door pockets wider than the door opening to fit these clearances.

    D · Hallway Elements

    • Assisted living: corridors must have handrails on both sides.
    • Independent living: corridors must have handrails on at least one side.
    • Handrail placement in other project areas is completely discretionary to individual design proposals.
    • Drinking fountains must be placed in the hallways following ADA standards.
      • Residential component: one drinking fountain for every 5 to 20 residents.
      • Educational administrative component: one drinking fountain for every 3,000 sq. ft.

    Level 05 of 08

    Furnishing, Fixtures & Tags

    Requirements for furniture, plumbing fixtures, room tags, and notes.

    A · FF&E, Casework & Fixed Systems

    • All three floors must be fully furnished.
      • Use loose furniture, cabinetry, and fixed furniture systems included in the Revit template where the Block Plans were developed. Do NOT create a separate Revit file.
      • If you cannot find the furniture, fixtures, or equipment you want in the template, download them from the Autodesk Cloud Library or bimobject.com.
    • Draw all furniture at true size, at 1/8″ scale.
      • Do not reduce the size of furniture to make a space work.

    B · Plumbing Fixtures

    • Draw all toilets, sinks, vanities, and showers at true size.
    • Draw their ADA clearances with dashed lines.
    • Draw a 5′-0″ turning circle in each accessible toilet room.
    • Follow the ADA Guide, Chapter 6: Toilet Rooms, and apply it in your project to comply with ADA standards.

    C · Room Tags

    • Tag every space with Space Name, Space Number, and Area in SF.
    • Start each room number with the floor number.
    • First Floor: 101, 102, 103.
    • Second Floor: 201, 202, 203.
    • Third Floor: 301, 302, 303.

    D · Annotations & Notes

    • Conserve and add code compliance notes for ADA, Building Code (IBC), and Fire / Life Safety compliance, including egress parameters.
    • Add partition notes: which walls are fire-rated, and their rating.
    • Add a note for any information the drawing does not show.

    Level 06 of 08

    Fire & Life-Safety Plans

    What the Fire & Life-Safety plans must show.

    Occupancy & Rated Construction

    • Add a reduced-scale diagram on the same sheet that color-codes occupancy by area.
    • Color-code rated walls by rating: 1-hour and 2-hour.

    Egress

    • Draw the egress and exit-access travel path to the most remote point and dimension it.
    • Mark dead-end corridors and their lengths.
    • Mark egress corridor widths.
    • Place compartment doors.

    Devices

    • Locate all of these on the plans.
    • Detection & alarm: smoke detectors and manual pull stations.
    • Notification: fire alarms.
    • Suppression & response: fire extinguishers.
    • Signage & lighting: exit signs and directional exit signs.

    Level 07 of 08

    Deliverables

    Sheets 1 and 2 · First, Second & Third Floors · 1/8″ = 1′-0″
    Interior Design Floor Plans
    • Draw the First, Second, and Third Floors at 1/8″ = 1′-0″.
      • The floor plans must include:
        • Accurate wall and partition types (fire-rated and non-fire-rated). These walls must be anchored (base level) to the corresponding structural level and reach up to the inferior face of the structural slab or roof above. Do this either by applying a negative offset equal to the structural slab or roof thickness, or by connecting the wall to the slab or roof above.
        • Windows (attached to the corresponding Floor Finish level) on the façade that support natural light and ventilation of interior spaces, considering the activities that take place in these spaces.
        • FF&E attached to the corresponding Floor Finish level.
        • Doors with swings attached to the corresponding Floor Finish level.
    • Use the same sheet size (36″ × 48″) as Assignment 3.
    • Include the Zoning Legend on Sheet 1 (First Floor).
    • Include the Design Statement on Sheet 1 (First Floor).
      • Value Proposition and Core Design Solution (modified if necessary) from Assignment 02.
      • Write a text that explains how the design concept remains evident in the detailed plans.
      • Explain how your interior design decisions appear in the plans.
      • Connect the Design Documents plans to the Concept Statement and to the research from Assignments 01 and 02.

    Level 08 of 08

    Evaluation Rubric

    How the 500 points are distributed across five criteria and three bands.

    Five Criteria · 500 Points

    • Spatial Translation & Code-Compliant Construction: 125 pts
    • Functional Resolution & Accessibility: 125 pts
    • Fire & Life-Safety Overlay Documentation: 125 pts
    • Technical Standards & Drawing Discipline: 75 pts
    • Concept Continuity & Design Statement: 50 pts
    • The three 125-point criteria carry the greatest weight.

    Three Bands

    • Excellent: 452–500 pts overall.
    • Proficient: 352–447 pts overall.
    • Developing: 0–347 pts overall.

    Graded as Developing

    • Functional Resolution: furniture is omitted or reduced in size to fit.
    • Technical Standards: the project template or the view templates are ignored.
    • Concept Continuity: the statement summarizes what was drawn instead of why.

    113 – 125 pts
    Furnishes the project honestly: proves every space functions by placing FF&E, casework, and plumbing fixtures at true size, and by drawing turning circles, clear floor spaces, and maneuvering clearances that demonstrate the building serves the people who will occupy it.
