    Level 01 of 08

    Overview & Warning

    What Assignment 04 is, what you must do before starting, and what you produce.

    What It Is

    • Assignment 04 turns your Assignment 03 Block Plan into Design Documents.
    • You draw the same plan at a higher Level of Detail (LOD).
    • Every space gets a name, a number, and an area.
    • Every wall gets a thickness and a fire rating.
    • Every room gets furniture and fixtures drawn at true size.
    • Design Documents are the set a client approves and a consultant coordinates against.

    Before You Start

    • Assignment 04 is built directly on your Assignment 03 Block Plan.
    • Stage 1 uses your block plan’s geometry. Only non-substantial edits and adjustments are allowed.
    • Stage 2 documents the fire and life safety of that same plan.
    • Check your Block Plan for unresolved egress paths, missing compartment lines, and mixed public–resident circulation.
    • Fix every deficiency first, before you start Assignment 04.
    • If you do not, the problems carry into both stages and you will have to redo the work.

    What You Produce

    • Floor plans that show the design is fully resolved.
    • Show walls with ratings, doors with correct swings, furniture, and fixtures with clearances.
    • Use notes and dashed clearances. Do not use dimensions.
    • The FF&E is a reference for scale, proportion, and functionality. It is not for a contractor to build from.
    • Use basic blocks or families. Do not spend time looking for detailed ones.

    At a Glance

    • Work: individual.
    • Stages: two.
    • Deliverables: three.
    • Scale: 1/8″ = 1′-0″.
    • Points: 500.

    Level 03 of 08

    Objectives & The Tools

    What the assignment asks you to do, what you bring to it, and the files you must work in.

    Objectives

    • Translate a block plan into accurate, code-compliant interior design floor plans.
    • Draw partitions, openings, and doors that carry their code performance as drawn information.
    • Demonstrate spatial functionality through FF&E, casework, and plumbing fixtures with real clearances.
    • Communicate compliance through room tags, clearances, and annotations rather than dimensions.
    • Translate the life-safety logic into a color-coded overlay a reviewer could check.
    • Sustain the design concept and the user research from schematic zoning into a resolved design.

    What You Bring

    • Your Block Plan from Assignment 03 — corrected, with every functional issue resolved.
    • The Concept Statement your firm (team) produced in Assignment 02.
    • The primary and secondary source research from Assignment 01, plus the course’s video repository.
    • The Area Program and the code requirements from Assignment 03. They remain unchanged; now they are drawn and specified, not diagrammed.
    • Software: AutoCAD or Revit, as your instructor directs for your section.

    When Using AutoCAD

    • A base drawing is provided: all layers, basic furniture blocks, dimensions, hatches, lettering, and 48″ × 36″ layouts with title blocks.
    • Use it as a template and adhere to its content.
    • No additional layers are allowed. Use the PURGE command to clear unwanted layers.
    • CTB files, one per Stage, are provided for accurate line widths and graphic standards.
    • Layers that are not native to the template cause plotting issues.
    • Load the CTB files with the PLOTTERMANAGER command, into the Plot Styles folder.

    When Using Revit

    • A Revit Project Template is provided.
    • It includes the View Templates for both stages.
    • It includes all required Revit Families.
    • It includes 48″ × 36″ Sheets with title blocks.

    Level 04 of 08

    Stage 1 · Walls, Windows & Doors

    Sheet 1 · Interior Design Floor Plans · First, Second, and Third Floors · 1/8″ = 1′-0″. Requirements for walls, windows, and doors.

    A · Walls & Partitions

    • Draw every wall between 4″ and 6″ thick. Walls under 4″ are not allowed.
    • Draw fire-rated partitions at least 5″ thick. A 4″ wall cannot be rated.
    • Use 2-hour walls at main hallways, assembly areas, egress stairs, janitor’s closets, technical / mechanical rooms, and storage rooms.
    • Use 1-hour walls at shared partitions and walls elsewhere.
    • Thickness does not show the rating. A 5″ or 6″ wall may or may not be rated.
    • Show the rating with notes on Sheet 1 and with color on Sheet 2.
    • Specify fire-rated wall assemblies using USG Wall Assembly types.

    B · Windows & Glass Partitions

    • Draw all exterior windows and interior glass partitions. Base them on the activities in each space.
    • Add notes to all interior glazing in a rated assembly.
    • Give glass partitions a fire rating where required (assembly spaces).
    • Specify fire-rated glass using Fireglass fire-rated products.

    C · Doors: Width & Swing

    • Draw every door with its swing, at its true width.
    • Resident bedrooms: 42″ minimum (gurney access).
    • All other doors: 36″ minimum.
    • Swing exit doors in the direction of egress.
    • Swing assembly doors out, without intruding into the corridor.
    • At each compartment line, draw barrier doors: a pair at 42″, each swinging in the opposite direction from the other.

    C · Doors: Clearance

    • Swing inward-opening doors against a wall or back surface, not into the space.
    • Outward-swinging doors may project no more than 7″ into a corridor.
    • Place doors at least 4″ from a back wall.
    • Leave 12″ on the push side and 18″ on the pull side.
    • Make door pockets wider than the door opening to fit these clearances.

    Level 05 of 08

    Stage 1 · Furnishing, Fixtures & Tags

    Sheet 1, continued. Requirements for furniture, fixtures, clearances, room tags, and notes.

    D · FF&E, Casework & Fixed Systems

    • Furnish every floor plan fully.
    • Use loose furniture, cabinetry, and fixed furniture systems (templates, blocks, or families).
    • Furnish every space in the program. Do not skip any.
    • Draw all furniture at true size, at 1/8″ scale.
    • Do not reduce the size of furniture to make a space work.

    E · Plumbing Fixtures

    • Draw all toilets, sinks, vanities, and showers at true size.
    • Draw their ADA clearances with dashed lines.
    • Draw a 5′-0″ turning circle in each accessible toilet room.
    • Toilets: centerline 18″ from the wall; 36″ and 42″ perpendicular grab bars; 56″ × 60″ clearance.
    • Roll-in showers: 30″ × 60″ clear space.
    • Sinks: 30″ × 48″ clear floor space in front.
    • Reference: ADA Guide, Chapter 6: Toilet Rooms.

    F · Clearances

    • Draw these: turning circles, clear floor spaces, maneuvering clearances at doors, and corridor widths.
    • Write these as notes: grab bar heights, knee and toe clearances, mounting heights, and reach ranges.

    G · Room Tags

    • Tag every space with Space Name, Space Number, and Area in SF.
    • Start each room number with the floor number.
    • First Floor: 101, 102, 103.
    • Second Floor: 201, 202, 203.
    • Third Floor: 301, 302, 303.

    H · Annotations & Notes

    • Add code compliance notes: under-counter and knee clearances, mounting heights.
    • Add partition notes: which walls are fire-rated, and their rating.
    • Add general notes: the occupancy classification key.
    • Add a note for any information the drawing does not show.

    Level 06 of 08

    Stage 2 · Fire & Life-Safety Overlay

    Sheet 2 · Fire & Life-Safety Overlay Plans · First, Second, and Third Floors · 1/8″ = 1′-0″. You draw the fire and life-safety information over your Stage 1 plans.

    The Underlay

    • Use your Stage 1 floor plans as the underlay.
    • Screen them to halftone (grey scale).
    • Draw all life-safety information on top, in full line weight and color.
    • The underlay must stay legible and must be lighter than the overlay.

    Occupancy & Rated Construction

    • Add a color-coded legend with the occupancy classification of each zone.
    • Add a reduced-scale diagram on the same sheet that color-codes occupancy by area.
    • Color-code rated walls by rating: 1-hour and 2-hour.
    • Match the ratings set on Sheet 1.

    Egress

    • Draw the egress and exit-access travel path to the most remote point. Dimension it.
    • Add direction-of-travel arrows.
    • Mark dead-end corridors and their lengths.
    • Mark egress corridor widths.
    • Mark compartment doors.

    Devices

    • Locate all of these on the plans.
    • Detection & alarm: smoke detectors and manual pull stations.
    • Notification: fire alarms.
    • Suppression & response: fire extinguishers and the fire department connection.
    • Signage & lighting: exit signs, directional exit signs, and emergency egress lighting.

    Legends & Symbology

    • Apply the provided CTB (AutoCAD) or View Template (Revit). It sets the color code.
    • Use the same color system on all floors.
    • Use only the symbols provided in the template files. Do not create your own symbols.

    Level 08 of 08

    Evaluation Rubric

    How the 500 points are distributed across five criteria and three bands.

    Five Criteria · 500 Points

    • Spatial Translation & Code-Compliant Construction: 125 pts
    • Functional Resolution & Accessibility: 125 pts
    • Fire & Life-Safety Overlay Documentation: 125 pts
    • Technical Standards & Drawing Discipline: 75 pts
    • Concept Continuity & Design Statement: 50 pts
    • The three 125-point criteria carry the greatest weight.

    Three Bands

    • Excellent: 452–500 pts overall.
    • Proficient: 352–447 pts overall.
    • Developing: 0–347 pts overall.

    Graded as Developing

    • Functional Resolution: furniture is omitted or reduced in size to fit.
    • Technical Standards: foreign layers are added or the plot styles are ignored.
    • Concept Continuity: the statement summarizes what was drawn instead of why.
