Section 01

Introduction

Until now, you have worked as a firm, as a team. From this point forward, the project becomes an individual endeavor. Assignment 03 is the first document produced individually, and it is where the project stops being an argument and becomes a building: a block plan carries the project’s functional DNA, where every function lives, how people move, how they get out, and how the public and resident worlds stay separate. Every student from the same firm (team) works from the same concept, the same program, and the same code, and every one of you will produce a different plan.

You do not arrive empty-handed. Bring the users you interviewed in Assignment 01, and make sure the design decisions in your plan relate to that primary-source research material; bring the collective library your class built across twenty-five topics, to navigate different programming challenges; bring the Concept Statement your firm (team) produced in Assignment 02, which should be legible in the way you arrange the plan; and bring the Mood Environments, which provide spatial characterization for programming in terms of scale, proportion, and dimension.

Section 03

The Block Plan is the Functional DNA of the Project

Before a single finish is chosen, the block plan decides how the building actually works — where each function lives, how people move, and how they get out. This is where a concept becomes a building you can occupy, and where code compliance shapes your design. It is better to address code compliance at an early stage of the project rather than try to fix it in place once it has taken on a more definitive shape. You are working at 1/8″ = 1′-0″ on purpose: the scale keeps you drawing zones and relationships and schematic fixture placement, not fixtures, detailed wood or millwork.

Section 06

The Block-Plan Checklist

What to draw and where to place it, so the plan is both code- and program-compliant. Open each section.

Resident Public / School Service Circulation / General
ASheet Setupwhat every block plan must show+
  • Two floor plans, First and Second, at the same scale and orientation, with a north arrow and graphic scale.
  • A zone legend: Resident, Public/External (School), Staff, Service, Circulation.
  • A life-safety symbol legend: exits, pull stations, extinguishers, exit signs, smoke detection, strobes, barrier doors.
  • Occupancy classification labeled per zone — I-1 Condition 2 (residential), Group B (school/admin), Group A-3 (assembly rooms ≥50).
  • Block area (SF) and preliminary occupant load noted in each block.
BFloor Assignmentwhere each cluster goes+
  • Place the School and the Administrative, Public & Service clusters entirely on the First Floor.
  • Place a self-sufficient residential cluster on each floor — each with its own sitting, dining, country kitchen, spa/bathing, laundry, and sunroom.
  • Distribute Central Activity & Program spaces across either floor, resident-side only.
  • Keep all resident zones out of the public school circulation on both floors.
CEntranceswhere people come in+
  • Place the main public entry on Floor 1, opening into the Lobby / Reception, so external students reach the School without crossing any resident zone.
  • Place a service / loading entry at back-of-house, adjacent to the kitchen and laundry/mechanical block.
  • Show at least one accessible entrance connected to accessible parking, with a vestibule air-lock.
DVertical Circulationwhere the cores land+
  • Draw the elevator + waiting area and two egress stairs, and align them vertically on both floors.
  • Place the two stairs toward opposite ends, separated by at least one-third of the floor’s diagonal.
  • Serve both the resident wing and the public wing without forcing cross-traffic.
  • Enclose each egress stair in 2-hour fire-rated construction, per this project’s fire-rating standard.
EEgresswhere the exits and corridors run+

What counts as an exit. Not every door is an exit. On a block plan an exit is a door that leads to safety — one of three kinds:

  • an exterior exit door to grade or the public way;
  • a door into an enclosed exit stair; or
  • a horizontal-exit door through a 1-hour barrier into an adjacent compartment that has its own exits — the “safe area.”

A door between two rooms, a corridor door, or a door into a single-exit room is not an exit.

  • Provide at least two exits from every story, and two from any space over 49 occupants (three above 500, four above 1,000).
  • Separate the two required exits by at least one-third of the served diagonal (sprinklered).
  • Position exits so exit-access travel from the most remote point is ≤ 75′ — show it dashed and dimensioned.
  • Let occupants move in two different directions from any point.
  • Give every assembly room (≥50) two exit doors, swinging out, clear of the corridor.
  • Confirm each smoke compartment reaches two exits, one of which may be a horizontal exit.
  • Draw the primary egress corridor (8′-0″ clear) as a spine; secondary corridors 44″ min (60″ to a 42″ door).
  • No dead-end over 20′-0″; no egress through a secondary space (except reception/waiting or kitchen-through-dining).
  • Construct the main hallway (primary egress corridor) walls as 2-hour fire-rated partitions, per this project’s fire-rating standard.
FCompartmentalizationwhere the defend-in-place line goes+

Group I-1 moves residents horizontally to a protected adjacent compartment rather than evacuating them at once. The barrier doors make that possible — their placement is graded.

This project’s fire-rating standard. Unless a specific item calls out a different rating: 2-hour fire-rated walls at main hallways (primary egress corridors), assembly areas, egress stairs, janitor’s closets, technical/mechanical rooms, and storage rooms; 1-hour fire-rated walls at shared partitions and walls elsewhere.

  • Draw a smoke/fire-barrier line dividing each residential floor into at least two compartments, running exterior wall to exterior wall (1-hour).
  • Size compartments so neither exceeds 22,500 SF and each can shelter both — allow ~15 SF per resident using a wheelchair/walker, 6 SF ambulatory.
  • Place barrier doors so travel to a barrier door ≤ 200′-0″, while still meeting the 75′ exit-access limit.
  • At the barrier, draw a pair of 42″ doors, each swinging opposite directions (the horizontal exit), central enough that both compartments stay viable refuges.
  • Draw the occupancy-separation line between the I-1 residential wing and the Group B school/admin areas as 1-hour rated.
GAccessible Routewhere barrier-free movement is guaranteed+
  • Draw a continuous barrier-free route from the accessible entry through every public space and to every resident room.
  • Place a 5′-0″ turning circle (dotted) at each change of direction and in each major public block.
  • Keep the accessible route clear of the service/loading path.
HService & Incidental-Use Roomswhere back-of-house sits+
  • The commercial kitchen is already located on the plans provided. Do not move it. Locate, relative to it, the service / loading entrance and the means of egress.
  • Place the service / loading entrance so deliveries reach the kitchen without passing through public or resident space.
  • Give the kitchen and service block egress discharging toward a back-of-house exit, each room a 1-hour enclosure.
  • Confirm the kitchen’s egress and service path do not cross the public school entrance sequence.
  • Group remaining back-of-house rooms into one service block on Floor 1. Enclose janitor’s closets, technical/mechanical rooms, and storage rooms in 2-hour fire-rated construction; enclose other back-of-house rooms in 1-hour construction.
  • Place the medication / staff workroom in the resident wing, a 1-hour room.
  • Locate mechanical/electrical off a service corridor, not through occupied space, and enclose it in 2-hour fire-rated construction (a technical room).
IProgram Adjacencieswhere clusters meet+
  • Cluster School spaces around the academic entry: reception, commons/café, wardrobe/locker, public restrooms near the door; classrooms, labs, studios, and the learning resource center deeper in.
  • Place the Lecture/Multipurpose Hall so its load exits toward a public exit, not through classrooms.
  • Enclose assembly areas (Great Room, Lecture/Multipurpose Hall) in 2-hour fire-rated walls, per this project’s fire-rating standard.
  • Place administrative offices adjacent to the lobby; place the conference room for staff and public reach.
  • Place the Wellness/Movement Studio on the resident–school boundary if shared; label which zone controls access.
  • Position the Administrative/Public/Service cluster to serve both the residences and the school — central, not buried in one wing.
JPublic–Resident Separationthe core design test+
  • Demonstrate two independent egress systems: school/public and resident loads reach exits without crossing.
  • Mark the controlled threshold where public and resident circulation meet.
  • Confirm external students never pass through a resident cluster to reach a classroom, restroom, or the commons.
KFire & Life-Safety Deviceswhere the NFPA elements go+

Manual fire alarm pull stations

  • Place a pull station within 5′-0″ of every exit door — each exterior exit, stair door, and horizontal-exit door.
  • Space pulls so travel to the nearest never exceeds 200′-0″.
  • Mount at accessible height (42″–48″ AFF).

    • Fire extinguishers

  • Max travel to one ≤ 75′-0″ (NFPA 10), and at least one per smoke compartment / egress zone.
  • One at each exit and stair, and at each hazard area — commercial kitchen (Class K), laundry, mechanical/electrical, and each resident-cluster kitchen.
  • On the accessible route, top ≤ 48″ AFF, never blocked.

    • Exit signs

  • An illuminated exit sign at every exit door.
  • Directional signs at every intersection and change of direction so no corridor point is > 100′-0″ from a visible sign.
  • Visible from any direction of egress travel.

    • Smoke & fire detection

  • Smoke detection at each barrier / horizontal-exit opening (releases hold-open doors).
  • Detection in corridors, resident sleeping areas, and assembly spaces; duct/area detection at mechanical rooms.
  • Align detection/alarm zones with your smoke compartments.

    • Notification (audible + visible)

  • Strobe coverage in all common/public spaces and within each resident room — age-related hearing loss makes audible-only alarms unreliable here.
  • Notification reaches every occupiable space, including toilet and spa/bathing rooms.

    • Areas of refuge

  • Fully sprinklered — a separate area of refuge is generally not required; the smoke compartment is the refuge. Note this rather than drawing redundant refuge areas.

Deliverables

The Block Plan · Individual Work

Before you start, your instructor will tell your section whether the block plan is hand-drawn or drafted in AutoCAD or Revit, and whether the set is submitted digitally or physically. Confirm both before you begin.

Deliverable 01 · Two Floor Plans · 1/8″ = 1′-0″ · Individual
The Block Plan

Draw First and Second Floors at the same scale and orientation, resolving five elements. Place partitions to define every room and corridor in the area program, dimensioned, and note each block’s area (SF) and preliminary occupant load. Place doors and egress routes, mark exits as exits, draw the 8′-0″ primary spine and secondary corridors, and show the longest travel path dashed and dimensioned. Place life-safety symbols — pull stations, extinguishers, exit signs, smoke detection, strobes, and the barrier doors — per the checklist, and key them to a legend. Add code notes explaining how the plan complies, covering occupancy classification per zone, travel distances, compartment sizes, separations, and any assumption you’re making. Add schematic, diagrammatic furniture test-fits that prove the proportions work — not a furniture plan.

Also required on the sheet: a north arrow, a graphic scale, a zone legend (Resident / Public-External / Staff / Service / Circulation), a life-safety symbol legend, and 5′-0″ turning circles shown dotted or dashed at changes of direction.

Medium. Hand-drawn or drafted in AutoCAD or Revit, as your instructor directs for your section. Either way the scale is true — a printed sheet must measure correctly at 1/8″ — and the conventions are the same: consistent line weights, a legible legend, and notes another professional could check.

Schematic example: illustrates the drawing conventions only, not a design solution for the actual project.

Deliverable 02 · Annotated · Submitted with the Plan
The Compliance Check
  1. Download the Compliance Checklist — alabama.box.com/s/v1iegi14mi8j46oriaa8zrhckmh3img6. The checklist may be printed, completed, and submitted physically, or completed as an Excel file and submitted digitally — this is instructor-dependent per section.
  2. Work through Sections A–K of the block-plan checklist and submit it with every box ticked, or with a note where an item doesn’t apply and why.
  3. This is not a formality. A ticked box you can’t defend at the wall is worse than an honest note. Where the plan required a judgment call — a compartment line placed here rather than there, a shared studio assigned to one zone — say so in a sentence.
Deliverable 03 · One Page · With the Plan
The Design Statement

One page connecting the scheme to everything behind it: how the plan serves the Concept Statement your firm produced in Assignment 02, with the Value Proposition legible in the arrangement rather than merely quoted at the top; how the plan serves the users you interviewed in Assignment 01, with at least one decision traceable to something a participant actually said; and the hardest conflict you resolved, and how — most likely the public-resident separation, the service path, or an egress route that fought the program.

Three short paragraphs are enough.

Format follows the plan. If your block plan is drafted in AutoCAD or Revit, the Design Statement must be produced in the same format — laid out on the sheet or as a matching sheet in the same file and set, not a separate document. If the plan is hand-drawn, the statement may be hand-lettered or typed, as your instructor directs.

Submission

Three items: the Block Plan, the Compliance Check, and the Design Statement.

Your instructor determines whether the set is submitted digitally or physically, and will confirm the method for your section. If digital, submit through Blackboard as a PDF at true scale, and the original DWG or RVT file. If physical, bring the printed or drawn set to the studio.

This is individual work. You share a concept with your team; you do not share a plan. Deadlines and any pin-up or review dates are on the course syllabus.

Evaluation Rubric

The assignment is evaluated across five criteria totaling 500 points. Spatial Definition & Space Planning and Life-Safety & Code Compliance carry the greatest weight, as they are the core technical work of the block plan.

Criterion Excellent Proficient Developing
1. Spatial Definition & Space Planning 150 pts total
135 – 150 pts
Translates the program with rigor: defines every space and circulation by resolving zones, areas, and adjacencies into a coherent scheme, conceptually and technically aligned.
105 – 134 pts
Translates the program competently: defines the required spaces by accommodating zones and areas in a workable scheme whose adjacencies function adequately.
0 – 104 pts
Translates the program loosely: defines spaces by disregarding areas, zones, or adjacencies, producing a scheme that places rooms next to each other without coherent functional considerations.
2. Life-Safety & Code Compliance 150 pts total
135 – 150 pts
Applies the building code, determines occupancy classification and load, and egress by governing the plan through compliant exits, travel distances, compartmentation, separations, and placement of life-safety devices.
105 – 134 pts
Applies the building code correctly, determines occupancy classification and load, and egress by satisfying the essential requirements while leaving isolated conflicts in travel distance, compartmentation, or device placement.
0 – 104 pts
Applies the building code superficially, determines classification and egress by misreading or omitting requirements, producing a plan with unresolved exits, travel distances, separations, or compartment lines.
3. Accessibility & Universal Design 80 pts total
72 – 80 pts
Applies universal design with precision: defines a continuous barrier-free route by demonstrating compliant clearances, turning circles, and door dimensions that serve this population throughout.
56 – 71 pts
Applies universal design adequately: defines an accessible route by meeting the required clearances and turning circles with isolated lapses.
0 – 55 pts
Applies universal design inconsistently: defines circulation by breaking the accessible route or by neglecting clearances, turning circles, and door dimensions.

0 – 41 pts
Plans for human factors superficially: asserts functionality by omitting test fits, thereby producing proportions that cannot accommodate their intended use.

54 – 60 pts

42 – 53 pts
Communicates the design clearly: documents code compliance by drawing to scale with adequate conventions and notes.
0 – 41 pts
Communicates the design poorly: documents code compliance by drawing off-scale or without legible conventions, legends, or notes.
