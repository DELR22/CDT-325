    Level 01 of 07

    Overview

    What Assignment 03 is, why it is drawn the way it is, and what you carry into it.

    What It Is

    • A block plan: the technical document that carries the project’s functional DNA.
    • It decides where every function lives, how people move, and how they get out.
    • It keeps the public and resident worlds separate.
    • Project: The Lifelong School. Client: NEXA Consulting and Development.

    Who Does It

    • Assignment 03 is the first individual document. Until now you worked as a firm (team).
    • Teammates share the same concept, the same program, and the same code.
    • Every student still produces a different plan.

    Scale & Timing

    • Drawn at 1/8″ = 1′-0″ — block scale, not detail.
    • The scale keeps you drawing zones, relationships, and schematic fixture placement.
    • It keeps you away from detailed fixtures, wood, or millwork.
    • Code compliance is addressed now, at the early stage — not fixed later once the plan has taken a definitive shape.

    What You Bring

    • Assignment 01 interviews — design decisions must relate to this primary-source research.
    • The class library — twenty-five topics, for navigating programming challenges.
    • Assignment 02 Concept Statement — legible in the way you arrange the plan.
    • Mood Environments — spatial character: scale, proportion, and dimension.

    Student Learning Outcomes

    Twelve outcomes in two frameworks. Each begins with the verb you will be assessed on.

    Bloom’s Taxonomy (2001)

    • Remember The purpose and scope of programming and block planning within the design process.
    • Understand How ADA compliance, occupancy classification, occupant load, and egress requirements determine the project’s functionality.
    • Apply And place partitions, doors, circulation, and life-safety elements to assess the scheme’s functionality and spatial coherence against both the program and the code requirements.
    • Analyze The conflicts a shared building creates to determine the variables for possible design solutions.
    • Evaluate The plan’s compliance by testing key project and code requirements.
    • Create A coherent, code-compliant three-floor block plan that carries the concept into a multi-documented functional scheme.

    CIDA / NASAD

    • Translate A program into a spatial scheme, allocating areas, zones, and adjacencies that serve the users and the concept.
    • Apply Building codes and life-safety regulations such as occupancy classification, occupant load, egress, fire separation, and compartmentation as design determinants.
    • Apply Universal design and ADA standards to produce barrier-free circulation, clearances, and accessible routes throughout.
    • Plan Space for human factors and behavior, testing proportion and function through schematic furniture fits.
    • Communicate A technical design through drawing conventions such as scaled drawings, symbols, dimensions, and notes.
    • Exercise Individual professional judgment by taking sole responsibility for a technical document and defending design decisions against code and project requirements.

    Level 03 of 07

    Step-by-Step Instructions

    The order of work, from the first question you ask your instructor to the Design Statement.

    1. Confirm the medium and the submission method. Your instructor tells your section whether the plan is hand-drawn or drafted in AutoCAD or Revit, and whether the set is digital or physical. Confirm both before you begin.
    2. Consult the reference documents.
      • Area program: go to Blackboard, in the Assignment folder.
      • Space requirements: go to Blackboard, in the Assignment folder.
    3. Set up three plans. First, Second, and Third Floors at 1/8″ = 1′-0″, at the same scale and orientation.
    4. Place partitions. Define every room, circulation, and common space in the area program. Dimension and proportion them. Tag each area program component with Name, Number, and Square Footage.
    5. Define wall types. Use 2-hour, 1-hour, and 0-hour walls for rooms, hallways, and common areas, based on inherent fire risk and construction code guidelines.
    6. Place doors and egress. Mark exits as exits.
      • Draw the 8′-0″ minimum hallways for assisted living and the 5′-0″ minimum hallways for independent living as primary egress paths leading to exits.
      • Secondary corridors must connect to these primary circulations.
      • Avoid creating dead-end corridors longer than 20′.
      • Show the longest travel path and dimension it.
    7. Place life-safety symbols. Pull stations, extinguishers, exit signs, smoke detection, strobes, and compartment doors — per the checklist, keyed to a legend.
    8. Add code notes. Explain how the plan complies: travel distances to egress and with compartment doors, and any clarification your project requires.
    9. Add furniture test-fits. Schematic and diagrammatic, to prove the proportions work. This is not a furniture plan, just a way of knowing your rooms work.
    10. Add turning circles and the legend. 5′-0″ turning circles (dashed) in corridors, bathrooms, common areas, and changes of direction.
    11. Run the Compliance Checklist. Work through Sections A–J as you draw, not after. Access the checklist at this link: alabama.box.com/s/v1iegi14mi8j46oriaa8zrhckmh3img6
    12. Write the Design Statement. On your first-floor Block Plan, write your concept statement — the Value Proposition and Core Design Solution from Assignment 2 — along with a text that clarifies how your proposal aligns with the concept statement and the design choices you make to make that happen.

    Schematic example: illustrates the drawing conventions only, not a design solution for the actual project.

    Level 04 of 07

    Project Constraints

    The numbers your plan is measured against. Room-by-room detail is in the reference documents on the next level.

    The Program

    • Total: 42,000 SF gross.
    • Residential & Shared Living: 21,004 SF — Floors 1, 2 & 3, resident access.
    • Central Activity & Program: 2,075 SF — Floor 1, 2 or 3 (any), resident access.
    • Academic & Learning (The School): 6,486 SF — Floor 1, public / external access.
    • Administrative, Public & Service: 5,295 SF — Floor 1, public / staff / service access.
    • Circulation, walls & gross-up: 7,140 SF.
    • Back entrance for mechanical maintenance and food deliveries.

    Corridors & Egress

    • Main egress route (main hallways) width: 8′-0″ minimum for assisted living; 5′-0″ minimum for independent living and other dependencies.
    • All paths of travel must lead to exits.
    • Secondary corridors: 44″ minimum.
    • Travel distance to a main corridor (common egress path) from any point, in any room on the floor: 75′ maximum in total.
    • Maximum travel distance to an exit: 200 ft without sprinklers, 250 ft with sprinklers.
    • No dead-end corridors longer than 20′-0″.
    • Egress stairs: minimum of 2 on each floor, at least 1/3 the diagonal of the floor apart with sprinklers; 1/2 the diagonal without sprinklers.
    • Egress paths cannot pass through secondary spaces subject to closure or storage.
    • Egress stairs: 48″ free circulation width, with railings.

    Doors

    • Egress doors open in the direction of travel.
    • Interior passage doors: 36″ minimum.
    • Resident room doors: 42″ minimum, to accommodate a gurney.
    • Apartment bathroom doors: 36″ minimum.
    • Outward-swinging doors can open onto the hallway 7″ maximum. They must be recessed to prevent swinging out any further.

    Fire Rating & Life Safety

    • 2-hour walls: main hallways, assembly areas, egress stairs, janitor’s closets, technical / mechanical rooms, and storage rooms.
    • 1-hour walls: shared partitions and walls elsewhere.
    • 0-hour walls: internal partitions inside rooms.
    • Compartmentalization is required between occupancy classes and, in residential, at least two separate compartments with an exit.
    • Also required: fire alarms, pull stations, exit signage, fire extinguishers, and security lighting.

    Accessibility

    • 5′-0″ turning circles at changes of direction.
    • Toilets: 16″–18″ clearance from the wall, with grab bars.
    • Barrier-free, ADA-compliant paths of travel throughout.

    Level 07 of 07

    Evaluation Rubric

    How the 500 points are distributed, and what separates the three performance bands.

    Five Criteria · 500 Points

    • Spatial Definition & Space Planning: 150 pts
    • Life-Safety & Code Compliance: 150 pts
    • Accessibility & Universal Design: 80 pts
    • Human Factors & Functional Test-Fit: 60 pts
    • Technical Communication & Professional Judgment: 60 pts
    • The two 150-point criteria weigh most: they are the core technical work of the block plan.

    Three Bands

    • Excellent: 450–500 pts overall. Rigorous, compliant, and coherent throughout.
    • Proficient: 350–445 pts overall. Workable, with isolated lapses or conflicts.
    • Developing: 0–345 pts overall. Requirements misread, omitted, or unresolved.
    • Example: omitting furniture test-fits places Human Factors in Developing.
    • Example: drawing off-scale or without legible legends and notes places Technical Communication in Developing.
